Documentation impact has always been hard to quantify, but the data to prove it exists. You just need to know where to look and what it is connected to. Here are the metrics worth tracking, and why each one matters beyond the docs team.

---

## Failed searches, your backlog already written

Every failed search is a user who came to your portal, typed a real question, and left without an answer. It is a timestamped documentation gap, no guessing required. When customers can not find answers, they contact support or a CSM. That is a measurable cost. Reducing failed searches is one of the clearest ways to connect documentation work to support deflection.

---

## Search-to-view conversion, the findability check

Good docs that no one can find have no value. This metric tells you whether your content surfaces when users go looking for it. Track it monthly and it becomes a trend: "conversion improved from 70% to 76%" tells leadership that self-service is improving and CSM capacity is being protected.

---

## Article drop-off, where the journey ends

Drop-off shows you where users stop reading mid-article. High drop-off midway through a procedure usually means the doc is incomplete or unclear, and users who hit a wall do not wait, they contact support. This metric separates docs that exist from docs that actually work.

---

## Coverage completeness, the go-live lens

A doc existing is not the same as a user being able to complete a task. Track coverage (does a doc exist?) and completeness (does it take the user all the way through?) separately, and connect both to implementation timelines. Gaps here are go-live risks, they surface post-launch as escalations, not internal reporting misses.

---

## Communication metrics, how your content actually lands

Release notes and documentation emails have their own data layer: open rate, click-through rate, deliverability. Together they form a funnel, sent to delivered to opened to clicked. A drop anywhere has a specific cause. Customers who do not read release notes miss feature updates, which leads to underutilisation and reactive support calls. Treat these numbers the way a marketing team treats campaign metrics.

---

## Workload and blocked status, team health as a delivery signal

A backlog growing faster than the team can clear means coverage gaps are accumulating. Blocked tickets mean work is sitting, and if a doc is blocked when a feature ships, that is an implementation readiness problem, not just a process issue. Velocity reported upward makes the case for capacity and planning investment before it becomes a crisis.

---

Every metric here connects to something leadership already measures: support cost, self-service capability, feature adoption, implementation readiness, customer engagement, delivery risk. The data is almost always already being collected. The job is to bring it together and use it to make decisions.

That is what turns a documentation team into a documentation strategy.
